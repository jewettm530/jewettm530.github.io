The research question

How do participants learn reward contingencies across repeated trials, how much do those learning patterns vary across individuals, and how might learning relate to stress and developmental differences?

Trial-level behavior + heart rate + EEG + session metadata → validated participant-session dataset → learning and stress analyses

Learning across trials

At the group level, participants moved above chance performance early in the task and then stabilized across later trials. The two sessions show similar overall learning patterns, although the fitted trajectories differ somewhat in their early rate of improvement.

Individual differences in learning

The group average hides substantial participant-to-participant variability. Some participants show rapid improvement, others show slower learning, and several remain near chance across much of the task.

Win-stay and lose-switch behavior

Choice behavior can also be described in terms of whether participants repeat a rewarded choice or switch after an unrewarded choice. Across the full sample, win-stay behavior was more common than lose-switch behavior in both sessions.

Exploratory learner-group comparison

I also examined win-stay and lose-switch behavior after dividing participants by an exploratory Session 1 learning parameter. These groups are useful for describing individual variability, but they should not be interpreted as fixed or clinically meaningful participant types.

Exploratory learner-group comparison. Win-stay and lose-switch tendencies shown separately for participants above and below the exploratory learning-group split.

Statistical caution: the learner-group classification is based on an exploratory Session 1 median split of a fitted learning parameter. Continuous analyses are generally more informative than treating these groups as fundamentally different participant types.

Research data pipeline

The repository is designed around reproducibility and data quality rather than simply producing a final graph. Participant/session matching, filename standardization, missing sessions, physiological-data availability, and data-quality issues are explicitly checked before downstream analyses.

  • JATOS behavioral-data conversion and standardization
  • Raw-data inventory and schema validation
  • Group and individual learning curves
  • Learning-rate and learning-slope analyses
  • Win-stay / lose-switch analysis
  • Heart-rate and EEG summaries
  • Integrated participant × session multimodal datasets
  • Developmental-group comparison workflows
